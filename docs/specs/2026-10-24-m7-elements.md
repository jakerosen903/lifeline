# M7 — Elements: moments on the timeline, everything else by what it is about

Status: redlined with the owner's decisions, 2026-10-25. Follows M6 (PR #9). Spec, not plan. The owner set the direction in conversation and answered every open call on 2026-10-25 ("Owner's decisions" at the end). Calls still marked **(my call)** are builder calls the owner did not overturn.

## Scope, and what this spec is not

The archive today has one element, the memory, and expects every one of them to have a date eventually. The owner's observation: much of what a person says is not a moment at all. It is a thing they had (a wall in their room, a first house, a first car), a place, a person, a thought or a perspective, or general information about their life. Forcing those onto a timeline makes the shelf fill with things that will never have a date and makes the app ask "when was this?" about things that never happened at a time.

M7 gives the archive four kinds of **entity** beside its **moments**, gives entities pages of their own, and makes the timeline one view among several. It does not add the spoken interviewer (M8), replay (M9) or photos (Phase 2). It does not reclassify anything without a tap.

## Ship order

M7 ships as two pull requests, in this order. The second one must not start merging until the first one is on `main`.

1. **PR A — Facts, feelings and goals leave the timeline** (Goal 1). Small: the `memory.kind` column (migration v9), the filters that read it, the About section, the Change sheet's "What is this?". No entity table. The owner feels the difference on the device before the entity work lands.
2. **PR B — Entities** (Goals 2 to 6). The entity model, the full person split, pages, entity cards, standing statements as details, ideas, domains, curiosity, packet and prompt v6 (migration v9b).

## Why now

Before the interviewer can ask "tell me about the car" there has to be a car to point at. Before replay can walk "your places," places need pages and spans. And the owner is already seeing the misfit: undated facts on the shelf, a resolver question about a sentence that is not an event.

## Goals

1. **Facts, feelings and goals leave the timeline first (PR A).** A memory the extractor marked `fact`, `feeling` or `goal` is not a moment. It is never shown as waiting for a date and never asked "when?". The resolver still places it: it computes and stores its range, and the timeline hides that range. It appears under "About" until Goal 3 makes it a detail of an entity.
2. **Four entities with pages.** People, Places, Things and Ideas. Possessions, buildings, vehicles, objects and made things are all Things. An entity is reached by what it is about: the People, Places, Things and Ideas screens, and from any moment that mentions it. Its page shows the entity's details first, in the person's words and dated by its span, then the moments it appears in.
3. **Standing statements attach to entities.** "The house had a blue door" is a detail of the Thing "the house"; "my grandmother was Portuguese" is a detail of the Person; "I believed hard work was everything" is a detail of an Idea. Facts, feelings and goals all move off the timeline into entity details. A statement about the person themself goes to **About you**, which grows out of the profile's free text.
4. **Ideas change over time.** An Idea keeps its moments in order, so "I used to think X" and "now I think Y" are one idea with two dated moments, not two unrelated memories. The app shows the order; it never judges the change.
5. **Domains as a second axis.** Home, family, work, health, travel, and whatever the person says, as free-text tags on any element, from hints the extractor hears. No fixed list.
6. **Curiosity asks for scenes.** "When?" only about moments. For entities, the question asks for one scene that involves it ("What do you remember first about the house on Elm Street?"), never for a date and never for an opinion.

## Non-goals and anti-goals

- No taxonomy the person has to learn. Four entity words, in plain English, and the card says which it heard.
- Nothing is reclassified silently. The extractor proposes; the card shows; a tap confirms or changes; older rows keep their old shape until a newer extractor version backfills them and the person sees the result.
- No entity without a telling behind it. Every entity is created from something the person said, with a span pointing at the words, like a memory.
- No question that asks the person to evaluate their own beliefs or feelings ("Do you still think that?", "Was that right?"). Principle 1: the app never evaluates a life. Principle 2: ask for scenes.
- The app never asks for a date on a Thing or a Place. The person can give one; the app does not prompt for it.
- A failure of M7 is a timeline that got emptier and a set of pages the person never opens. Entities must be one tap from the moments that mention them and back.

## Goal 1 — Facts, feelings and goals leave the timeline (PR A)

- **Which kinds.** `fact`, `feeling` and `goal` are not moments ("we lived on Elm Street", "I was terrified of that dog", "I always wanted to be a pilot"). In this section, "a non-moment" means a memory of one of those three kinds. `event` and `other` stay moments.
- **Timeline.** A non-moment is left out of the shelf and the chapters. A new section "About" lists them (title, the quote). The summary line counts them as "and n things about your life".
- **Resolver: placed but hidden.** The resolver places a non-moment like any memory: it computes and stores a range when the memory carries anchors ("we lived in Lisbon from 1990 to 1995"). The timeline does not show that range, no chapter row or derived chapter range includes it, and no `unresolved_anchor` question or relative question is made about it. A non-moment already in a chapter keeps its `chapter_id` and leaves the chapter's rows and derived range. Goal 2 reads the stored range for the entity's span.
- **No "when?" on a non-moment.** "Add a date" and "Tell me when" are not offered on it.
- **Cards.** The `fact` card already reads "Something about your life"; Keep lands it under About. Nothing else changes.
- **Data.** A `kind` column on `memory` (migration v9), filled from each memory's card payload; a floating or payload-less memory is `event`. Every filter reads the column, never the payload **(my call: reading the payload from five places turns a one-day step into a week)**.
- **Correction.** The Change sheet gains "What is this?": a moment, something about your life, a feeling, something you wanted. Changing it rewrites the column; a moment turned non-moment leaves the shelf and its questions, a non-moment turned moment comes back. No kind changes without that tap.
- This step ships as PR A, before the rest of M7.

## Goal 2 — Entities (PR B)

**Model.** One table `entity (id, type: person | place | thing | idea, title, detail_text, created_from_candidate_id, created_at, hidden_from_timeline, excluded_from_ai)` and one link table `moment_entity (memory_id, entity_id, relation: detail | mention, role_text)`. Every page, merge, separate, delete and packet path reads that one link table. An entity has `spans` like a memory (`entity_span`), so its first telling plays.

**The person split goes all the way (owner's decision 4). This is the biggest task in M7.** People become entities with the single link table. There is no parallel path: `memory_person` migrates into `moment_entity` with `relation = mention`, as `memory_place` does, and no code reads `memory_person` after v9b. `person` keeps only what only a person has: `entity_id`, `is_owner`, birth date and its origin, notes. The name becomes `entity.title`; `PeopleRepository.rename` keeps writing the old name as an alias. The owner gets an entity of their own; "About you" is its details. `insertPerson`, Who's-this and the Change sheet write the entity row first, then the person row. `hidden_from_timeline` and `excluded_from_ai` on an entity mean what they mean on a memory: out of browsing; never in a packet. Every caller of the people tables (the matcher, the packet, merge, separate, delete, the People screen, Who's-this, backup and restore) moves in the same PR. The plan must give this its own tasks and its own migration test on a populated v8 database.

**Span of an entity.** Derived from everything linked to it: the earliest and latest of the ranges of its moments and details, with their uncertainty ("between 1993 and 1998" when they are guesses). This includes the stored-but-hidden ranges of non-moments (Goal 1).

**A stated span on a Thing or a Place is person-initiated only (owner's decision 7).** The entity page offers "Add dates" as a quiet action, like "Add a date" on a memory. The person opens it; the app never asks. It writes the seven range columns on the `entity` row with origin `stated`, and a stated span wins over the derived one. No curiosity item, card, opening prompt or hint asks for an entity's dates.

**Naming an entity (my call).** `MemoryOut` gains `entity_refs: [{ text, kind: place | thing | idea, likely_entity_id }]` beside `people_refs`; `place_text` stays one release and is read as a place ref. Mentions gain a `ref` so `about_ref` can point at one, and `likely_entity_id` from the packet, the analogue of `likely_alias_of_person_id`. A ref with a `likely_entity_id` on file links; otherwise it matches an entity of the same type by normalized title (as places match today) and creates one if none matches. The embedding matcher is not involved in M7.

**Idea titles are the person's words (red-flag fix).** An Idea's title must come from the owner's speech. The extractor proposes a title only when it can quote at most 8 consecutive words the person said, verbatim, from the segment. Otherwise it proposes no title, and the app titles the idea "An idea you mentioned on {date}", with the date of the telling ("An idea you mentioned on 25 October"). The person can edit the title on the card and on the page. The extractor never composes, paraphrases or summarizes a title for an idea. The output validator rejects an idea title that is not a substring of the segment's words (normalized for case and punctuation) and replaces it with the default.

**Places and things on Keep (my call).** A place or thing named on a kept memory's card (its chips) is linked on Keep without a card of its own: Keep includes what the card showed. The "Sounds like a place / thing / something you think" card is for a mention that no memory on the card carries. Migrated places take the primary span of the first memory that linked them, so their page can play.

**Pages.** Each entity has a screen, in this order (owner's decision 6): the title; the details in the person's words (the standing statements, Goal 3); the span; Play of its first telling; then the moments it appears in (as timeline rows); then the people, places and things that co-occur. Entry points: the People screen (exists), new Places, Things and Ideas screens from the timeline toolbar, and tapping an entity chip on a memory. **(my call)** The four screens are one screen with a segmented control (People, Places, Things, Ideas), not four tabs.

**Cards.** The extractor's mention types grow from `person | place` to `person | place | thing | idea`. A new "Sounds like a thing you had" / "Sounds like a place" / "Sounds like something you think" card follows the who's-this card's shape: the phrase, Keep / Not quite, and for a thing or place a span of the words that describe it. A card that names an entity already on file links instead of creating.

**Resolving a mention** already links every memory in the segment that named the person; the same holds for things, places and ideas. "Not quite" on an entity card offers the other types ("It's a place", "It's a person", "It's a thing", "Something I think") and "Not any of these", the way Who's-this offers someone new and "not a person"; a wrong type is one tap to fix, never a dismiss.

## Goal 3 — Standing statements attach to entities (PR B)

Facts, feelings and goals all become entity details (owner's decision 1). The extractor's `fact`, `feeling` and `goal` kinds gain an optional `about_ref`: the entity (from the packet) or the mention (from this output) the statement is about, or `self` for the person. Keep writes the memory with its span as today and links it to the entity with `moment_entity.relation = detail` (a moment that merely names the entity links with `relation = mention`; one memory may do both for different entities). The entity's page shows details first, moments after. `self` links the detail to the owner's entity, which is what "About you" lists; a non-moment with no `about_ref` has no link and sits under About. The person moves a detail between entities, or to About you, from the memory's Change sheet ("About whom or what?"); that sheet is also the only path for non-moments kept before v6, since V1 has no in-app re-run.

**Details and the rest of the machinery (my call).** A detail is embedded and retrieved like a memory, and takes part in "Are these the same?" pairs, so a fact told twice merges into one. It does not join stories and is not offered as a story member; stories are for scenes.

## Goal 4 — Ideas change over time (PR B)

An Idea's page orders everything linked to it, details and moments, by range when there is one and by the date it was told otherwise, and shows each one's quote as one line, so the change reads top to bottom. The extractor is told: a thought or belief the person states about themselves, the world, or how to live is an `idea` mention; a moment where they say it changed is a moment linked to the same idea. No sentiment analysis, no summary of what the person "really" thinks, no question about whether the person still holds the idea.

## Goal 5 — Domains (PR B)

Domains are free-text tags (owner's decision 8). A `domain_tag (element_kind, element_id, text)` table **(my call)**. The extractor's `domain_hint` on a memory or mention yields a hint card like the collection hint ("Sounds like it belongs to: home"). The person can type any tag. Tags filter the timeline and the entity screens. There is no fixed list. Nothing is tagged without a tap.

## Goal 6 — Curiosity asks for scenes (PR B)

New curiosity kinds, each with one plain question that asks for a scene:

| Kind | When it opens | Question |
|---|---|---|
| `entity_without_moment` | a place or thing with details but no linked moment | "What do you remember first about {title}?" |
| `person_without_moment` | a person named only in mentions, with no moment that has them in a scene | "Is there one time with {name} that stands out?" |
| `idea_without_moment` | an idea with no linked moment | "Is there a moment that brought that to mind?" |

None of them asks "when?", asks for a date, or asks the person to judge, rate or re-evaluate a belief or a feeling. "When?" questions keep their rule: moments only. **(my call)** In M7 these items exist, are owned by the resolver run like `unresolved_anchor` (made and retired in the same replace step), carry payoff 1 so they can lead an opening but never outrank a dated question, close when a linked moment arrives or when dismissed, and reach the person only as the next session's opening prompt. The tappable question with an answer waits for the interviewer (M8).

## Packet and prompt

The context packet carries entities with their type and a one-line detail (budgeted like memories, after people). Extractor prompt v6 adds the `thing` and `idea` mention kinds, `about_ref`, `domain_hint`, and these rules:

- "A thing, place or idea named in the segment is a mention, not a memory. A sentence that describes it is a fact about it."
- "An idea's `title` must be words the person said, copied exactly, at most 8 words. If no short run of their words names the idea, leave `title` null. Never write a title in your own words."
