# M7 — Elements: moments on the timeline, everything else by what it is about

Status: draft for the owner's review, 2026-10-24. Follows M6 (PR #9). Spec, not plan. The owner set the direction in conversation; calls the owner did not make are marked **(my call)** so they can be overturned while reading.

## Scope, and what this spec is not

The archive today has one element, the memory, and expects every one of them to have a date eventually. The owner's observation: much of what a person says is not a moment at all. It is a thing they had (a wall in their room, a first house, a first car), a place, a person, a thought or a perspective, or general information about their life. Forcing those onto a timeline makes the shelf fill with things that will never have a date and makes the app ask "when was this?" about things that never happened at a time.

M7 gives the archive four kinds of **entity** beside its **moments**, gives entities pages of their own, and makes the timeline one view among several. It does not add the spoken interviewer (M8), replay (M9) or photos (Phase 2). It does not reclassify anything without a tap.

## Why now

Before the interviewer can ask "tell me about the car" there has to be a car to point at. Before replay can walk "your places," places need pages and spans. And the owner is already seeing the misfit: undated facts on the shelf, a resolver question about a sentence that is not an event.

## Goals

1. **Facts leave the timeline today.** A memory the extractor marked `fact` (standing information, not an event) is never shown as waiting for a date, never asked "when?", and never placed by the resolver. It appears under "About" until Goal 2 gives it a proper home. This is the one-day step; it ships first.
2. **Four entities with pages.** People (exist), Places (exist, gain a page and a span), Things (new), Ideas (new). An entity is reached by what it is about: the People, Places, Things and Ideas screens, and from any moment that mentions it. Its page shows the entity itself first, described in the person's words and dated by its span, then the moments it appears in **(my call, the owner's open question)**.
3. **Standing statements attach to entities.** "The house had a blue door" is a detail of the Thing "the house"; "my grandmother was Portuguese" is a detail of the Person; "I believed hard work was everything" is an Idea. A statement about the person themself goes to **About you**, which grows out of the profile's free text.
4. **Ideas change over time.** An Idea keeps its moments in order, so "I used to think X" and "now I think Y" are one idea with two dated moments, not two unrelated memories.
5. **Domains as a second axis.** Home, family, work, health, travel, and whatever the person says, as free-text tags on any element, from hints the extractor hears. No fixed list in V1.
6. **Curiosity asks the right question.** "When?" only about moments. For entities: "Who else lived there?", "What happened to the car?", "Do you still think that?"

## Non-goals and anti-goals

- No taxonomy the person has to learn. Four entity words, in plain English, and the card says which it heard.
- Nothing is reclassified silently. The extractor proposes; the card shows; a tap confirms or changes; older rows keep their old shape until a newer extractor version backfills them and the person sees the result.
- No entity without a telling behind it. Every entity is created from something the person said, with a span pointing at the words, like a memory.
- A failure of M7 is a timeline that got emptier and a set of pages the person never opens. Entities must be one tap from the moments that mention them and back.

## Goal 1 — Facts leave the timeline (the one-day step)

- **Timeline.** A memory whose card kind is `fact` is left out of the shelf and the chapters. A new section "About" lists them (title, the quote). The summary line counts them as "and n things about your life".
- **Resolver and curiosity.** A `fact` memory is never placed and never the subject of an `unresolved_anchor` question or a relative question. "Add a date" and "Tell me when" are not offered on it.
- **Cards.** The `fact` card already reads "Something about your life"; Keep lands it under About. Nothing else changes.
- **Data.** A `kind` column on `memory` (migration v9a), filled from each memory's card payload; a floating or payload-less memory is `event`. Every filter reads the column, never the payload **(my call: the builder check showed that reading the payload from five places turns a one-day step into a week)**.
- **Correction.** The Change sheet gains "What is this?": a moment, something about your life, a feeling, something you wanted. Changing it rewrites the column; a moment turned fact leaves the shelf and its questions, a fact turned moment comes back. No kind changes without that tap.
- **Not only `fact`.** `feeling` and `goal` are not events either ("I was terrified of that dog", "I always wanted to be a pilot"); they leave the shelf and the "when?" questions with `fact`, and sit under About until Goal 3 gives them a home. **(owner's call 6)**
- **A fact with a date.** The resolver still computes and stores a range on a fact that carries anchors ("we lived in Lisbon from 1990 to 1995"); the timeline does not show it and nobody is asked about it, but Goal 2 reads it for the entity's span. A fact already in a chapter keeps its `chapter_id` and leaves the chapter's rows and derived range.
- This step ships as its own small PR before the rest of M7, so the owner feels the difference.

## Goal 2 — Entities

**Model.** One table `entity (id, type: person | place | thing | idea, title, detail_text, created_from_candidate_id, created_at, hidden_from_timeline, excluded_from_ai)` and one link table `moment_entity (memory_id, entity_id, role_text)`. `person` and `place` rows migrate into it: the `person` table stays as the owner of aliases, birth date and mentions, and gets an `entity_id`; `place` becomes an entity type **(my call: one page model for all four beats three parallel ones; the person table's extras stay where they are)**. An entity has `spans` like a memory (`entity_span`), so its first telling plays.

**Span of an entity.** Derived, never asked: the earliest and latest of the ranges of everything linked to it, moments and details alike, with their uncertainty ("between 1993 and 1998" when they are guesses). A Thing or Place may also carry a *stated* span from "Tell me when" phrased as "From when to when did you have it?"; it lives in the same seven range columns a memory has, on the `entity` row, and a stated span wins over the derived one **(my call)**.

**The person split goes all the way (my call, owner's call 7).** `memory_person` migrates into `moment_entity` with `relation = mention` as `memory_place` does, so every page reads one link table and merge, separate, delete and the packet keep one path. `person` keeps what only a person has: `entity_id`, `is_owner`, birth date and its origin, notes; the name becomes `entity.title` and `PeopleRepository.rename` keeps writing the old name as an alias. The owner gets an entity of their own; "About you" is its details. `insertPerson`, Who's-this and the Change sheet write the entity row first, then the person row. `hidden_from_timeline` and `excluded_from_ai` on an entity mean what they mean on a memory: out of browsing; never in a packet.

**Naming an entity (my call).** `MemoryOut` gains `entity_refs: [{ text, kind: place | thing | idea, likely_entity_id }]` beside `people_refs`; `place_text` stays one release and is read as a place ref. Mentions gain a `ref` so `about_ref` can point at one, and `likely_entity_id` from the packet, the analogue of `likely_alias_of_person_id`. A ref with a `likely_entity_id` on file links; otherwise it matches an entity of the same type by normalized title (as places match today) and creates one if none matches. The embedding matcher is not involved in M7. An Idea needs a short title the segment rarely contains ("I believed hard work was everything" is the detail, not the title): the extractor composes one of at most 8 words, the card shows it, and Keep is the person's acceptance, exactly as a memory's title works today.

**Places on Keep (my call).** A place or thing named on a kept memory's card (its chips) is linked on Keep without a card of its own: Keep includes what the card showed. The "Sounds like a place / thing / something you think" card is for a mention that no memory on the card carries. Migrated places take the primary span of the first memory that linked them, so their page can play.

**Pages.** Each entity has a screen: the title, the detail in the person's words (the standing statements, Goal 3), the span, Play of its first telling, the moments it appears in (as timeline rows), and the people, places and things that co-occur. Entry points: the People screen (exists), new Places, Things and Ideas screens from the timeline toolbar, and tapping an entity chip on a memory. **(my call)** The four screens are one screen with a segmented control, not four tabs.

**Cards.** The extractor's mention types grow from `person | place` to `person | place | thing | idea`. A new "Sounds like a thing you had" / "Sounds like a place" / "Sounds like something you think" card follows the who's-this card's shape: the phrase, Keep / Not quite, and for a thing or place a span of the words that describe it. A card that names an entity already on file links instead of creating.

**Resolving a mention** already links every memory in the segment that named the person; the same holds for things, places and ideas. "Not quite" on an entity card offers the other types ("It's a place", "It's a person", "Something I think") and "Not a thing", the way Who's-this offers someone new and "not a person"; a wrong type is one tap to fix, never a dismiss.

## Goal 3 — Standing statements attach to entities

The extractor's `fact`, `feeling` and `goal` kinds gain an optional `about_ref`: the entity (from the packet) or the mention (from this output) the statement is about, or `self` for the person. Keep writes the memory with its span as today and links it to the entity with `moment_entity.relation = detail` (a moment that merely names the entity links with `relation = mention`; one memory may do both for different entities). The entity's page shows details first, moments after. `self` links the detail to the owner's entity, which is what "About you" lists; a fact with no `about_ref` has no link and sits under About. The person moves a detail between entities, or to About you, from the memory's Change sheet ("About whom or what?"); that sheet is also the only path for facts kept before v6, since V1 has no in-app re-run.

**Details and the rest of the machinery (my call).** A detail is embedded and retrieved like a memory, and takes part in "Are these the same?" pairs, so a fact told twice merges into one. It does not join stories and is not offered as a story member; stories are for scenes.

## Goal 4 — Ideas change over time

An Idea's page orders everything linked to it, details and moments, by range when there is one and by the date it was told otherwise, and shows each one's quote as one line, so the change reads top to bottom. The extractor is told: a thought or belief the person states about themselves, the world, or how to live is an `idea` mention; a moment where they say it changed is a moment linked to the same idea. No sentiment analysis, no summary of what the person "really" thinks.

## Goal 5 — Domains

A `domain_tag (element_kind, element_id, text)` table **(my call)**. The extractor's `domain_hint` on a memory or mention yields a hint card like the collection hint ("Sounds like it belongs to: home"). Tags filter the timeline and the entity screens. Nothing is tagged without a tap.

## Goal 6 — Curiosity

New curiosity kinds: `entity_without_span` (a place or thing with no dated moment), `person_without_relation`, `idea_without_change`. Each has one plain question. "When?" questions keep their rule: moments only. **(my call)** In M7 these items exist, are owned by the resolver run like `unresolved_anchor` (made and retired in the same replace step), carry payoff 1 so they can lead an opening but never outrank a dated question, close when a linked moment or detail arrives or when dismissed, and reach the person only as the next session's opening prompt. The tappable question with an answer waits for the interviewer (M8).

## Packet and prompt

The context packet carries entities with their type and a one-line detail (budgeted like memories, after people). Extractor prompt v6 adds the `thing` and `idea` mention kinds, `about_ref`, `domain_hint`, and the rule: "A thing, place or idea named in the segment is a mention, not a memory. A sentence that describes it is a fact about it."

## Owner's calls to confirm

1. Facts leave the timeline first, as its own small PR (with the `kind` column and the Change-sheet correction), before the entity work.
2. Four entity types: person, place, thing, idea. Possessions, buildings, vehicles, objects and made things are all "things".
3. An entity page shows the thing itself first, its moments after.
4. Domains are free-text tags, no fixed list in V1.
5. `place` folds into `entity`; `person` keeps a small table for what only a person has.
6. **Feelings and goals leave the timeline with facts.** "I always wanted to be a pilot" and "I was terrified of that dog" are not events; they go under About (later: details of an entity or of you). The alternative is to move only facts and keep asking "when?" about wishes and feelings.
7. **The person split goes all the way**: people become entities like places and things, one link table for everything, the name on the entity. The alternative is a shallower change (people keep their own link table and name) that leaves two code paths for every page and merge.
